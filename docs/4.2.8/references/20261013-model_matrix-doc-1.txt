TL;DR: This specification defines a 10-tier agentic routing and asymmetric auditing matrix
grounded in the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3.2 evaluation suite (October 2026
baseline), mapping 11 distinct task types across Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google models with 5
ranked fallback slots per tier. It corrects frontier model hierarchy assumptions based on live
benchmark telemetry, establishes normalized score floors per task type, resolves composite
multi-domain tasks via weighted capability-to-cost scoring, and enforces cross-provider
asymmetric verification.
1. Corrections to Baseline Assumptions & Routing
Mechanics
Direct analysis of the live Artificial Analysis v4.3.2 leaderboards requires three corrections to the
proposed model hierarchy and ranking logic before constructing the matrix:
  ●    Frontier Ceiling Correction (Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 vs. Fable 5.1, and Astra Max
       vs. Astra Ultra):
   ○    Anthropic: Claude Fable 5.1 (Max) (Intelligence Index: 53, Cost per Task: $7.63) is no
        longer Anthropic's top-scoring model. Both Claude Opus 5.5 (Max) (Index: 58, Cost per
        Task: $5.98) and Claude Sonnet 5.5 (Max) (Index: 56, Cost per Task: $5.46) score higher
        while costing less per task. In general routing, Fable 5.1 (Max) is Pareto-dominated by
        Opus 5.5 (Max). However, Fable 5.1 (xHigh/Max) retains a specialized slot in Tiers 9–10
        for tool-heavy research (Humanity's Last Exam with tools) and long-horizon coding loops
        with heavy context reuse.
   ○    OpenAI: OpenAI's reasoning effort schema uses six levels (non-reasoning, low, medium,
        high, xhigh, max). There is no GPT-6 Astra Ultra endpoint; the top configuration is GPT-6
        Astra (Max) (Index: 53, Cost per Task: $3.26).
  ●    Google's Mid-Tier Capability Gap (Gemini 3.8 Flash to Gemini 4 Argon):
   ○    Google's lineup exhibits a structural gap between Gemini 3.8 Flash (High) (Overall Index:
        41, Cost per Task: $1.24, Speed: 145 tok/s) and Gemini 4 Argon (High) (Overall Index:
        53, Cost per Task: $1.99).
   ○    As anticipated in your requirements, Gemini 3.8 Flash (High) covers a wide span (Tiers
        3–6) for Coding, Long-Context Extraction, and General tasks (scoring 73.8% on
        DeepSWE v1.1, within 0.3 points of GPT-6 Astra), but hits a hard ceiling on Scientific
        Reasoning (HLE, CritPt) and Complex Knowledge Work (AA-Briefcase) above Tier 5.
        Consequently, Google must elevate Gemini 4 Argon (High) early starting at Tier 6 for
        those task types, while OpenAI (GPT-6.1 Sol across low/medium/high/xhigh/max) and
        Anthropic (Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 across effort levels) smoothly populate Tiers
        5–8.
  ●    Resolving the Intra-Tier Ranking Conflict (Capability Floor vs. Cost Optimization):
   ○    Ranking models inside every tier purely by 1. Capability and 2. Price without an upper tier
        bound would cause Claude Opus 5.5 (Max) to win Tier 1 through Tier 10, collapsing the
        matrix.
   ○    To preserve a true 10-tier progression while honoring both criteria, routing uses a
        Two-Stage Constrained Lexicographic Rule:

    1.  Stage 1 (Hard Floor & Effort Band Gate): A candidate model 𝑚 must achieve a task
        score 𝑆𝑚,𝑡 greater than or equal to the tier's minimum score floor 𝐹𝑘,𝑡 for task type 𝑡 in tier
        𝑘, and belong to the tier's designated cost-per-task band (unless a provider lacks any
        lower-tier model meeting 𝐹𝑘,𝑡, triggering Provider Tier Elevation).
    2.  Stage 2 (Intra-Band Capability-First, Cost-Second Ordering): Among models within
        the active tier band that clear 𝐹𝑘,𝑡, candidates are ranked first by Task Capability Score
        (𝑆𝑚,𝑡) and second by Lowest Cost per Task (𝐶𝑚,𝑡) (with Value Efficiency η𝑚,𝑡                =  𝑆𝑚,𝑡/𝐶𝑚,𝑡
        used to assign the primary slot when two models fall within the benchmark's $\pm
        1.5\%$ confidence interval).

        tier.
6.2 Asymmetric Audit Model Selection Matrix
Using the same model family to generate and review an output introduces correlated blind spots
and self-preference bias (where LLM judges rate their own family's formatting higher). The
matrix deterministically assigns an Asymmetric Auditor (M_audit) using three rules:
   1.  Strict Provider Orthogonality: 𝑃𝑟𝑜𝑣𝑖𝑑𝑒𝑟(𝑀𝑎𝑢𝑑𝑖𝑡)                 ≠  𝑃𝑟𝑜𝑣𝑖𝑑𝑒𝑟(𝑀𝑒𝑥𝑒𝑐), where 𝑀𝑒𝑥𝑒𝑐 is the
       model slot (M1–M5) that actually produced the output.
   2.  Tier Step-Up (k + 1 Verification): For tasks executed at Tiers 1–9, the auditor is selected
       from Tier     min  (10,  𝑘 +   1) (or a lower-effort variant of a frontier family whose knowledge

       floor exceeds Tier 𝑘, such as GPT-6.1 Sol or Claude Opus 5.5 Low/Medium) to ensure the
       verifier has strictly higher discrimination capability than the generator without paying Tier
       10 generation token costs (since auditing requires high input/cache tokens but low output
       tokens).

PAGE 27
  3.   Tier 10 Orthogonal Frontier Cross-Audit: At Tier 10, where no k + 1 tier exists, the
       matrix pairs complementary frontier strengths:
   ○   Claude Opus 5.5 (Max) output → Audited by GPT-6 Astra (Max) (leverages Astra's #1
       CritPt formal verification and GDP.pdf grounding) or Gemini 4 Argon (High) (for
       AutomationBench API compliance and SciCode unit verification).
   ○   GPT-6 Astra (Max) output → Audited by Claude Opus 5.5 (Max) (leverages Opus's #1
       AA-Omniscience anti-hallucination score of 46 and AA-Briefcase rubric rigor).
   ○   Gemini 4 Argon (High) output → Audited by Claude Opus 5.5 (High/Max) or GPT-6
       Astra (Max).
